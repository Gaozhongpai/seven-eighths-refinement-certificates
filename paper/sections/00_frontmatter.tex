\begin{abstract}
We refine OpenAI's zero-free half-plane for all Dirichlet $L$-functions
and all finite-order Hecke $L$-functions over $\Q(\sqrt{-3})$.
The boundary is $2187392879/2500000000=0.8749571516$, with principal poles
allowed. The proof is conditional on OpenAI's unrefereed preprint: it
uses its seven-eighths theorem as an input and reuses its construction
and estimates under modified parameters, with a hypothesis audit for
each reused statement. We first change the averaging scales in its
cubic-theta probe and obtain $20999/24000$. We then extend the range
of its plain-moment estimate and let the prime-slot capacity use this
improved boundary. Two finite applications give the stated result.
The proof retains the residue, tail, height and normalization costs;
the recursive moment argument is supplied in a companion proof note.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{gex:introduction}

Throughout, $F=\Q(\sqrt{-3})$. OpenAI \cite{OAI26} gives a proof, in an
unrefereed preprint, that every Dirichlet $L$-function and every
finite-order Hecke $L$-function over $F$ is zero-free in $\Rea s>7/8$.
Its proof compares a cubic-theta
character sum with a Mellin signal containing $1/L$. We retain this
construction, change the averaging scales, and verify that its plain
moment can use a slightly wider parameter range. The latter allows
the comparison to use the boundary already obtained in the preceding
step.

\begin{theorem}\label{gex:main}
Put $\theta_*=2187392879/2500000000=0.8749571516$.
Every finite-order Hecke $L$-function over
$F$ and every Dirichlet $L$-function, including imprimitive
presentations, is zero-free in $\Rea s>\theta_*$, with principal poles
at $s=1$ excepted. In particular this holds for the Riemann zeta
function. Every zero $\rho$ with $0<\Rea\rho<1$ of one of these
$L$-functions satisfies
\[
 \frac{312607121}{2500000000}\le\Rea\rho
       \le\frac{2187392879}{2500000000}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The boundary is independent of the conductor and height. Constants
and thresholds in the character-sum estimates may depend on the
fixed target. The proof uses \cite[Theorem~1.1]{OAI26}, namely
$\beta_*\le7/8$ in the notation of Section~\ref{gex:high-section},
as a hypothesis, together with the statements of \cite{OAI26} listed
in Table~\ref{gex:input-table}. Theorem~\ref{gex:main} is therefore
conditional on the correctness of those statements.

The proof begins with the following intermediate boundary.
\begin{proposition}\label{gex:first-stage}
Under the same external inputs, every member of the stated families
is zero-free in $\Rea s>\theta_0=20999/24000$, with principal poles
at one excepted.
\end{proposition}
For Sections~\ref{gex:source-section}--\ref{gex:recovery-section},
write $\theta=\theta_0$. The extra prime length lowers the direct
estimate by $1/24000$
while leaving the principal exponent $s-11/16$ unchanged. It also
changes the estimate for the remaining rows. Retaining the
perturbation in OpenAI's two-parameter endpoint certificate
\cite[Lemma~20.2]{OAI26} gives a quadratic that is positive
throughout the parameter rectangle. The
resulting margin pays the change of scales and the recovery errors.
Section~\ref{gex:adaptive-section} then proves Theorem~\ref{gex:main}
by extending the plain-moment range and using each established
all-height boundary as input to the next step. The construction never
uses the output boundary in its own moment estimate.

Sections~\ref{gex:source-section}--\ref{gex:low-recovery}
establish the low estimate. Section~\ref{gex:high-section} proves
the endpoint bound, and Section~\ref{gex:recovery-section} completes
the first-stage recovery and continuation.
Section~\ref{gex:adaptive-section} gives the two adaptive steps;
the full recursive moment proof is in \cite{GaoAdaptive26}.
Section~\ref{gex:limit-section} records the limits of the parameter
refinement and the scope of the verification archive. Appendix~\ref{gex:audit}
audits the hypotheses of every reused statement.

The reused inputs are listed in Table~\ref{gex:input-table}. The new
arguments are the modified-scale low estimate, the re-run endpoint
certificate at the perturbed geometry, the admission of the
principal Euler correction below $7/8$, and the extended plain-moment
range with its finite feedback. No improved long inverse moment is
assumed or proved here.
